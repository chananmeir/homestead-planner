# A YEAR IN THE GARDEN — A USER'S JOURNEY THROUGH HOMESTEAD PLANNER

*Full-spectrum homesteader edition. This version assumes you're running the app the way a serious year-round grower uses it: continuous succession planting across many crops, four-season harvest via cold frames and a high tunnel, overwintered crops planted in fall for spring harvest, and microgreens/sprouts during the darkest weeks. The garden is never dormant.*

---

## PREFACE — THE YEAR-ROUND MINDSET

Before walking through the calendar, understand the shift: this isn't "plant in May, harvest in September, rest all winter." That's a hobby calendar. A real homesteading calendar has **four overlapping streams running constantly**:

1. **Succession streams** — short-cycle crops (radish, lettuce, spinach, cilantro, beans, carrots) sown every 10–21 days so something is always ready
2. **Main-season stream** — long-cycle warm crops (tomato, pepper, squash, corn) planted once (or in 2 staggered waves) in late spring
3. **Four-season harvest stream** — cold-hardy crops (mache, kale, claytonia, winter spinach, tatsoi) planted in late summer/early fall to reach 75% size before the Persephone Period, then held alive through winter under protection for weekly harvest
4. **Overwintered stream** — crops planted in fall that sit dormant through winter and resume growth in spring (garlic, overwintering onions, spring spinach)

The app's settings that make this possible:
- **Season extension** on each bed (cold frame, low tunnel, high tunnel, greenhouse, row cover, cloche)
- **Succession preference** on each garden plan item (0–8 scale, per-seed overrides)
- **Succession interval days** and succession count per crop
- **Soil temperature with mulch modeling** to predict when soil is warm enough
- **Future Plantings Overlay** to visualize every upcoming wave without placing them yet
- **MIGardener Row Planner** for beds using row-based method — schedule succession row-by-row

A "Persephone Period" is Eliot Coleman's term for the window each winter when daylight drops below 10 hours and plant growth effectively pauses. In zone 5b, that's roughly November 7 → February 7. During this window, crops in your high tunnel don't grow — but they hold alive and harvestable. The app's soil temperature card and planting date calculations assume this reality: anything that needs to reach harvestable size before Persephone must be sown by mid-August at the latest.

Now let's walk the year.

## HOW SUCCESSION PLANTING ACTUALLY LIVES IN THE APP

To pull it out of the narrative: succession planting isn't a one-time setup. It's a weekly rhythm reinforced by the app:

1. **Plan level** — when you set "12 successions at 14-day intervals" for a crop in the Garden Planner wizard, the app creates 12 PlantingEvents linked by `succession_group_id` (a shared UUID).
2. **Calendar level** — those 12 events appear on the Planting Calendar on their scheduled dates.
3. **Dashboard level** — each event surfaces as "Direct Seed Due" 1–3 days before its date.
4. **Execution level** — you mark each succession done when you sow it.
5. **Quantity level** — total plan quantity divides across successions (720 radishes total → 60 per succession).
6. **Space level** — the Row Planner for MIGardener beds shows each row's succession timeline as a horizontal strip; the Future Plantings Overlay on SFG beds ghosts future waves.
7. **Date-aware counting** — the sidebar plant-count shows plants **expected in-ground today**, not full-season totals; active successions compute dynamically.
8. **Harvest level** — date-aware harvest windows predict when each succession will come in, spreading the peak.

What you personally do is walk out the door, sow the row, come back in, and tap "mark done." The app does the arithmetic.

## HOW YEAR-ROUND GROWING ACTUALLY LIVES IN THE APP

Four infrastructure things and four scheduling things have to cooperate:

**Infrastructure (configured once per bed):**
1. Season extension setting per bed: cold frame, low tunnel, high tunnel, greenhouse
2. Layer count (single vs double poly, with or without row cover inside)
3. Mulch type (affects soil temperature modeling)
4. Shade cloth for summer

**Scheduling (handled by the plan):**
1. Four-season crop plantings timed backwards from Nov 7 to reach 75% size
2. Overwintered crops (garlic, spring spinach, onions) placed in September-October
3. Winter harvests logged weekly from tunnel/frame contents
4. Cover crops on beds that are truly "off" for the winter

The app supports all eight. Most users set up infrastructure in the Property/Bed designers once, then the scheduling flows from the Garden Planner wizard's per-crop configuration. The dashboard, the soil temperature card, and the harvest tracker do the rest.

## THE KEY INSIGHT, REVISED

The app's shape reveals itself over a year:

- The **Dashboard** is the front door and changes character with the season — January shows winter harvests + planning, April shows germination checks + transplants, August shows harvests + fall plantings, November shows weekly tunnel cuts.
- The **Garden Planner** wizard isn't about "this summer" — it's about the full 12 months including winter-hold crops, overwintered crops, and cover crops.
- **Succession planting** is supported at every layer: plan, calendar, dashboard alerts, spatial layout (Row Planner, Future Plantings Overlay), date-aware sidebar counts.
- **Year-round production** depends on the bed season-extension settings being honest and the plan including the four streams (succession, main-season, four-season, overwintered).
- The **three-model backbone** (Garden Plan → Planting Events → Planted Items) keeps your intention, your schedule, and your reality in sync every week of the year.

A full year in the app, used this way, produces a dense, queryable, photographed, seed-saving, nutritionally-quantified record of a genuinely self-sufficient homestead that meaningfully feeds the household through all 52 weeks — not just the easy 20 weeks between May and September.
